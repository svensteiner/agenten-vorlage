# Content draft

Extract audience, desired action, channel and supported product claims. Ask only for facts
that materially affect the draft; use stated assumptions for reversible stylistic choices.
Write the actual deliverable. Verify links and claims where source access is available.
Flag placeholders for unavailable facts. Return the draft path and what was checked.
Sending or publication is a separate operation and is not granted by loading this skill.

For X, write one concrete idea with a specific opening and a useful implication,
within the supplied character limit. Avoid a miniature summary of every source fact,
generic hype, or invented personal experience. Keep a source reference for each factual
claim beside the draft, outside the publishable text.

Distinguish what the source states, what follows from it, and what is only a conditional
proposal. A question mark or “I suggest” does not justify unsupported advice. A proposal's
condition and rationale must follow from cited evidence without inventing actions, risks,
mandatory controls or outcomes. Missing measurements prove neither failure nor success.
Preserve human checks and approvals required by the source; do not suggest removing them
or imply they are optional.

After editing, hand the exact final text to the independent reviewer. The reviewer checks
facts, supported implications, format and the actual character count; style preferences
alone should not trigger another rewrite of a valid text. A required change creates a new
draft and requires fresh review. Report rejected or missing outputs as failures rather
than dropping them from a quality comparison. Internal scores do not prove audience reach.
